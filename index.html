Valuation Context

INTU currently trades at a forward P/E of ~16–21× — dramatically below its 5-year median of ~59× and its historical range of 30–60×. At the all-time high of $813 (Jul 2025), the stock was pricing in years of perfect execution across every business unit.


The market is now pricing in significant risk to TurboTax's long-term revenue from AI-native competitors. Goldman Sachs projects TurboTax revenue could be 18% lower by 2030 vs. 2025 levels, assuming just 20% of DIY filers migrate to AI alternatives.


The consensus analyst target of ~$491 (50%+ upside) and GF Value estimate of ~$800 both suggest a potential overcorrection at current prices — if the business proves more resilient than Goldman's bear case. The key variable: TurboTax's 2027 tax season performance.

🏢 Workforce & Layoff History
Intuit's record of mass workforce reductions — critical context for evaluating management's capital allocation and business trajectory
Mid-2022 — Targeted restructuring (estimated ~600–900 roles)
As the broader tech sector began pulling back from pandemic-era hiring, Intuit quietly eliminated some positions through attrition and targeted cuts. Not a formally announced mass layoff — individual team reductions in areas deemed lower priority relative to cloud and platform strategy.
January 2023 — ~1,800 employees (approximately 10% of global workforce)
Part of the broader 2022–23 Big Tech wave (Amazon, Google, Meta, Salesforce all cut simultaneously). CEO Goodarzi framed it as "aligning resources to strategic priorities" — specifically AI-first product development and away from administrative overhead. The company was still growing at the time; this was about margin improvement and re-focusing, not business distress.
Context: INTU stock was ~$400–450 range · Company still guiding for double-digit growth
🚨 May 20, 2026 — ~3,000+ employees (17% of global workforce) — THIS ROUND
Announced simultaneously with Q3 FY2026 earnings that missed TurboTax revenue guidance. On the same call, CEO Goodarzi admitted "we lost on price" and "we did not have the tax season we expected."
Offices named for closure:
📧 Mailchimp Atlanta, GA (the original Mailchimp HQ — acquired for $12B in 2021)
· Reno, NV · Woodland Hills, CA
Stock: ~$270 close · Single-session decline: −20% · Down 62% from ATH ($813, Jul 2025)
2023 vs 2026 — Critical distinction: The 2023 round was industry-wide belt-tightening while the company was still growing. The 2026 round is performance-driven — triggered by actual revenue miss, a structural competitive threat (AI replacing TurboTax), and the underperformance of the $12B Mailchimp acquisition. Structural problems are significantly harder to recover from than cyclical cost-cutting.

Total layoffs since 2022: ~6,000–7,000+ employees across multiple rounds — representing roughly one in four of the workers Intuit employed at its 2022 peak headcount.
Why Insider Transactions Matter
Under SEC rules, all officers and directors must file a Form 4 within 2 business days of any trade in company stock. These are public filings — they are not optional and they are not private.

Large insider sells near price peaks are historically one of the most reliable warning signals available to ordinary investors. The catch: most people don't monitor them in real time.

The INTU case study: On January 7, 2026, CEO Sasan Goodarzi sold approximately 40,960 shares at ~$650/share (~$26.6M total) via a pre-planned 10b5-1 trading rule. Four and a half months later, on May 20, 2026, INTU fell 20% in a single session. Had you been watching Form 4 filings, you could have sold alongside him near the top. This tool's dashboard alerts you the moment any qualifying insider sell appears in live data.
